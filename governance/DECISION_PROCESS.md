# AI-Toolkit Decision Process

Version: 1.0.0

Status: Draft

Classification: Canonical Governance Document

Owner: AI CTO

---

# Introduction

This document defines the official engineering decision process of the AI-Toolkit ecosystem.

The objective is to ensure that architectural, engineering and governance decisions are transparent, traceable, evidence-based and reproducible.

Engineering decisions are considered canonical artifacts and shall follow the same governance principles as every other engineering asset.

---

# Objectives

The decision process shall:

- eliminate arbitrary decisions
- preserve architectural integrity
- maintain complete traceability
- support engineering evidence
- enable reproducible evolution
- ensure accountability

---

# Decision Principles

Every engineering decision shall be:

- justified
- documented
- measurable
- reviewable
- auditable
- reversible whenever practical

---

# Decision Categories

AI-Toolkit recognizes the following decision categories:

Strategic Decisions

Architectural Decisions

Engineering Decisions

Implementation Decisions

Operational Decisions

Release Decisions

Governance Decisions

Each category follows the same governance lifecycle while differing in scope and authority.

---

# Decision Lifecycle

Every decision progresses through the following stages:

Idea

↓

Architecture Requirement (AR)

↓

Initial Analysis

↓

Architecture Audit

↓

Architecture Decision Record (ADR)

↓

Impact Analysis

↓

Approval

↓

Roadmap Planning

↓

Implementation

↓

Validation

↓

Audit

↓

Release

↓

Continuous Review

No engineering decision shall bypass this lifecycle.

---

# Decision Inputs

Engineering decisions may originate from:

- strategic objectives
- architecture evolution
- audit findings
- implementation feedback
- validation evidence
- operational metrics
- security analysis
- contributor proposals

---

# Required Evidence

Every decision shall include, where applicable:

- problem statement
- motivation
- constraints
- alternative solutions
- selected solution
- expected benefits
- implementation impact
- migration considerations
- compatibility analysis
- identified risks

---

# Decision Evaluation

Before approval, every proposal shall be evaluated according to:

architectural consistency

engineering quality

canonical alignment

implementation feasibility

maintainability

security implications

performance implications

long-term sustainability

---

# Approval Authority

Decision authority depends on decision category.

Strategic decisions require Project Owner approval.

Architectural decisions require Architecture Board approval.

Engineering decisions require AI CTO approval.

Implementation decisions may be delegated when they do not alter canonical specifications.

---

# Decision Outputs

Approved decisions may produce:

new standards

standard revisions

ADR updates

roadmap updates

architecture modifications

implementation work

validation activities

audit activities

documentation updates

---

# Traceability

Every decision shall maintain traceable links to:

Architecture Requirements

Architecture Audits

Architecture Decision Records

Canonical Standards

Implementations

Validation Reports

Audit Reports

Release Documentation

---

# Decision Quality

Engineering decisions shall prioritize:

correctness over speed

evidence over opinion

architecture over convenience

long-term sustainability over short-term optimization

canonical consistency over local optimization

---

# Continuous Improvement

The decision process shall evolve through measurable engineering evidence.

Lessons learned from audits, implementations and operational experience shall improve future decision making.

---

# Closing Statement

The AI-Toolkit Decision Process establishes a disciplined engineering methodology in which every significant decision becomes an auditable, traceable and reusable engineering asset.

Engineering excellence depends not only on making good decisions, but on making them through a consistent and transparent process.
