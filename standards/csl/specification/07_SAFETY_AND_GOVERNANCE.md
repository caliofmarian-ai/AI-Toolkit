# Canonical Specification Language (CSL)

# Volume VII

# SAFETY AND GOVERNANCE

Version: 1.0.0

Status: Normative

Classification: Core Specification

---

# Chapter 1

Purpose

The Safety and Governance Specification defines the mandatory rules that ensure every conforming implementation remains safe, controllable, auditable and accountable.

No implementation of the Canonical Specification Language shall execute engineering actions without governance.

Safety is mandatory.

Governance is mandatory.

Human authority is mandatory.

---

# Chapter 2

Objectives

The Safety and Governance framework shall:

Protect Canonical Knowledge.

Protect Human Authority.

Protect Engineering Integrity.

Prevent unauthorized execution.

Prevent uncontrolled automation.

Provide complete traceability.

Guarantee accountability.

Support deterministic execution.

---

# Chapter 3

Fundamental Principle

Automation exists to assist humans.

Automation never replaces engineering responsibility.

Human authority shall always remain superior to automation.

Artificial Intelligence assists.

Humans decide.

---

# Chapter 4

Safety Architecture

Every conforming implementation shall include:

Permission Engine

↓

Risk Engine

↓

Approval Engine

↓

Audit Engine

↓

Execution Engine

↓

Monitoring

↓

Emergency Stop

No execution shall bypass this architecture.

---

# Chapter 5

Permission Model

Every executable action requires permissions.

Permissions shall be explicit.

Permission categories include:

Read

Create

Modify

Delete

Approve

Compile

Generate

Deploy

Execute

Administer

Permissions may be granted only through governance.

Implicit permissions are prohibited.

---

# Chapter 6

Roles

Minimum roles include:

Viewer

Contributor

Reviewer

Maintainer

Administrator

Owner

Implementations may introduce additional roles.

Roles determine permissions.

Roles do not determine engineering truth.

---

# Chapter 7

Risk Classification

Every executable action possesses a Risk Level.

Minimum levels include:

LOW

MEDIUM

HIGH

CRITICAL

Risk classification determines approval requirements.

Risk levels shall remain transparent.

---

# Chapter 8

Approval

Approval shall be explicit.

Approval records shall include:

Approver

Timestamp

Engineering Object

Risk Level

Decision

Justification

Critical actions require explicit human approval.

Automatic approval of critical actions is prohibited.

---

# Chapter 9

Artificial Intelligence

Artificial Intelligence shall never possess unrestricted authority.

Artificial Intelligence may:

analyze,

recommend,

generate,

validate,

refactor,

summarize.

Artificial Intelligence shall never:

approve itself,

override governance,

modify Canonical Knowledge without authorization,

disable safety mechanisms.

---

# Chapter 10

Audit

Every executable action shall produce an audit record.

Audit records shall include:

Timestamp

Actor

Action

Risk Level

Approval Status

Execution Result

Related Engineering Objects

Audit records are immutable.

Audit records shall never be silently removed.

# Chapter 11

Execution Authorization

No executable engineering action shall begin without authorization.

Authorization shall verify:

Identity

Permissions

Role

Risk Classification

Approval Status

Policy Compliance

Safety Constraints

Unauthorized execution is prohibited.

Authorization failures shall terminate execution.

---

# Chapter 12

Execution Policies

Execution Policies define organizational governance.

Policies may regulate:

Compilation

Generation

Deployment

Repository Modification

Runtime Configuration

Infrastructure Changes

Artificial Intelligence Usage

Policies shall be versioned.

Policies shall be reviewable.

Policies shall be traceable.

---

# Chapter 13

Emergency Stop

Every conforming implementation shall provide an Emergency Stop capability.

Emergency Stop immediately suspends:

Compilation

Generation

Execution

Automation

Artificial Intelligence Operations

Background Tasks

Emergency Stop shall never destroy Canonical Knowledge.

Emergency Stop shall preserve audit history.

---

# Chapter 14

Monitoring

Safety monitoring shall continuously observe:

Compiler Activity

Generator Activity

Approval Events

Permission Changes

Risk Events

Execution Results

Monitoring shall never modify engineering semantics.

Monitoring produces observations only.

---

# Chapter 15

Governance Events

Every governance decision becomes a Governance Event.

Governance Events include:

Approval

Rejection

Permission Grant

Permission Revocation

Policy Change

Emergency Stop

Emergency Resume

Risk Override

Governance Events become permanent Engineering Objects.

---

# Chapter 16

Policy Validation

Every execution request shall validate applicable policies.

Validation verifies:

Permission Rules

Risk Rules

Approval Rules

Compliance Rules

Execution Rules

Validation failures prevent execution.

---

# Chapter 17

Accountability

Every engineering action shall possess accountable ownership.

Accountability identifies:

Responsible Human

Executing System

Approval Chain

Affected Engineering Objects

Execution Outcome

Artificial Intelligence shall never become accountable.

Accountability remains human.

---

# Chapter 18

Security Principles

Every implementation shall preserve:

Confidentiality

Integrity

Availability

Authenticity

Traceability

Least Privilege

Explicit Authorization

Defense in Depth

Security mechanisms shall never violate Canonical Knowledge.

---

# Chapter 19

Governance Conformance

An implementation conforms to this specification only if it:

implements permissions,

implements approvals,

implements audit logging,

implements risk classification,

implements emergency stop,

implements execution authorization,

implements governance validation.

Unsupported governance capabilities shall be explicitly declared.

---

# Chapter 20

Closing Statement

Safety and Governance protect Human Authority.

Automation accelerates engineering.

Governance controls automation.

Canonical Knowledge remains protected.

Every future implementation of the Canonical Specification Language shall preserve these principles.
