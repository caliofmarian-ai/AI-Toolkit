# Canonical Specification Language (CSL)

# CONSTITUTION

Version: 1.0.0

Status: Normative

Classification: Foundational Standard

---

# Preamble

This Constitution establishes the supreme governing principles of the Canonical Specification Language (CSL).

The Constitution defines the immutable engineering laws upon which the CSL ecosystem is built.

Every future specification, compiler, validator, engineering tool, generator, runtime and reference implementation shall conform to this Constitution.

No future document shall contradict this Constitution.

If a conflict exists between this Constitution and any other CSL document, this Constitution shall prevail.

---

# Article I

Authority

Canonical Knowledge is the highest engineering authority.

Documentation is not authoritative.

Source code is not authoritative.

Architecture diagrams are not authoritative.

Issue trackers are not authoritative.

Artificial Intelligence outputs are not authoritative.

Generated artifacts are not authoritative.

Canonical Knowledge alone possesses engineering authority.

Every engineering artifact derives its authority exclusively from Canonical Knowledge.

---

# Article II

Purpose

The Constitution exists to ensure that engineering knowledge remains stable while implementations continue to evolve.

Technology evolves.

Programming languages evolve.

Artificial Intelligence evolves.

Infrastructure evolves.

Repositories evolve.

Engineering knowledge remains.

The Constitution protects engineering knowledge from implementation changes.

---

# Article III

Objectives

The Constitution establishes the following objectives.

Preserve engineering knowledge.

Prevent knowledge duplication.

Guarantee deterministic engineering.

Protect human authority.

Enable continuous evolution.

Ensure traceability.

Guarantee safety.

Support interoperability.

Maintain implementation independence.

Protect long-term compatibility.

Every conforming implementation shall pursue these objectives.

---

# Article IV

Scope

The Constitution governs:

The CSL language.

The CSL grammar.

The Universal Engineering Model.

Engineering Compiler implementations.

Validation Engines.

Engineering Generators.

Safety and Governance mechanisms.

Reference Implementations.

Future Standards.

RFC governance.

No subsystem exists outside the authority of this Constitution.

---

# Article V

Engineering Authority

Authority flows in only one direction.

Human Vision

↓

Human Intent

↓

Canonical Knowledge

↓

Engineering Compiler

↓

Universal Engineering Model

↓

Engineering Artifacts

↓

Execution

No lower layer may redefine a higher layer.

Implementations never redefine Canonical Knowledge.

Generated artifacts never redefine engineering intent.

Artificial Intelligence never redefines human objectives.

---

# Article VI

Human Authority

Only humans define engineering purpose.

Humans approve engineering intent.

Humans approve engineering policy.

Humans approve engineering safety.

Humans approve engineering governance.

Artificial Intelligence assists engineering.

Artificial Intelligence does not own engineering knowledge.

Artificial Intelligence does not become accountable.

Artificial Intelligence remains an engineering assistant.

Human responsibility is absolute.

---

# Article VII

Canonical Knowledge

Canonical Knowledge possesses the following mandatory characteristics.

It is complete.

It is deterministic.

It is reviewable.

It is version controlled.

It is machine readable.

It is human readable.

It is traceable.

It is reproducible.

It is implementation independent.

It is technology independent.

If any engineering information lacks these characteristics it shall not be considered Canonical Knowledge.

---

# Article VIII

One Source of Truth

Engineering knowledge shall exist exactly once.

Any duplicated engineering statement represents architectural debt.

Generated artifacts shall never become independent sources of engineering truth.

Canonical Knowledge remains the only authoritative engineering source.

Every implementation shall minimize duplicated engineering information.

---

# Article IX

Semantics

Meaning always has priority.

Syntax exists only to express meaning.

No implementation may sacrifice semantic correctness for syntactic simplicity.

Whenever syntax and semantics conflict,

semantics shall prevail.

The Universal Engineering Model represents semantic reality.

Everything else represents semantic projections.

---

# Article X

Implementation Independence

The Canonical Specification Language shall remain independent of implementation technologies.

The standard shall not depend upon:

Programming Languages

Frameworks

Repository Platforms

Cloud Providers

Operating Systems

Databases

Artificial Intelligence Providers

Deployment Technologies

Future implementations remain free to choose implementation technologies provided semantic compatibility is preserved.

# Article XI

Deterministic Engineering

Engineering shall be deterministic.

Given:

the same Canonical Knowledge,

the same compiler version,

the same compiler configuration,

the same validation rules,

the resulting Universal Engineering Model shall be identical.

Consequently,

the generated engineering artifacts shall be semantically identical.

Random engineering behavior is prohibited.

Random engineering decisions are prohibited.

Artificial Intelligence shall never introduce nondeterministic engineering knowledge into the canonical model.

---

# Article XII

Engineering Compiler

The Engineering Compiler is the only component authorized to transform Canonical Knowledge into Engineering Artifacts.

Every conforming Engineering Compiler shall perform the following conceptual stages.

Knowledge Acquisition

↓

Lexical Analysis

↓

Parsing

↓

Semantic Analysis

↓

Universal Engineering Model Construction

↓

Validation

↓

Optimization

↓

Artifact Generation

↓

Verification

↓

Publication

Generators shall never operate directly upon source documents.

Generators shall always operate upon the Universal Engineering Model.

---

# Article XIII

Universal Engineering Model

The Universal Engineering Model (UEM) represents the canonical semantic structure of every engineering project.

The Universal Engineering Model shall be independent of:

programming languages,

repository structures,

documentation formats,

Artificial Intelligence providers,

operating systems,

cloud providers,

deployment technologies.

Every conforming implementation shall preserve semantic equivalence with the Universal Engineering Model.

No engineering artifact may redefine the Universal Engineering Model.

# Article XV

Validation

Every Canonical Knowledge revision shall be validated before compilation.

Validation includes:

syntactic validation,

semantic validation,

structural validation,

dependency validation,

policy validation,

governance validation,

compatibility validation.

Compilation shall not proceed if validation fails.

Validation failures shall be explicit.

Silent validation failures are prohibited.

---

# Article XVI

Safety

Safety is mandatory.

Every conforming implementation shall provide mechanisms for:

permission control,

risk classification,

human approval,

execution auditing,

rollback support where technically feasible,

emergency stop,

execution isolation.

Safety shall always take precedence over automation.

Automation shall never bypass safety mechanisms.

---

# Article XVII

Risk Classification

Every executable engineering action shall possess a risk classification.

The minimum classifications are:

LOW

MEDIUM

HIGH

CRITICAL

Critical actions require explicit human approval.

Implementations may introduce additional classifications.

Risk classifications shall remain transparent.

---

# Article XVIII

Human Approval

Certain engineering operations require human approval before execution.

Examples include:

production deployment,

destructive operations,

repository deletion,

credential modification,

permission escalation,

financial operations,

security policy modification.

Human approval shall be explicit.

Implicit approval is prohibited.

Automatic approval of critical actions is prohibited.

---

# Article XIX

Auditability

Every engineering action shall produce an audit record.

Audit records shall include at minimum:

timestamp,

actor,

origin,

requested action,

executed action,

approval status,

risk classification,

execution result.

Audit information shall remain immutable after publication.

Engineering systems shall preserve audit history.

# Article XX

Transparency

Engineering systems shall remain transparent.

Every generated engineering artifact shall disclose:

its canonical origin,

its compiler version,

its generation timestamp,

its validation status,

its originating Canonical Knowledge,

its Engineering Model version.

Opaque engineering artifacts are prohibited.

Generated artifacts shall always remain explainable.

No compiler decision shall become permanently hidden.

---

# Article XXI

Traceability

Every engineering decision shall remain traceable.

Every requirement shall identify its origin.

Every generated issue shall identify its originating requirement.

Every generated source file shall identify its originating engineering model.

Every generated test shall identify the engineering rules it validates.

Every deployment specification shall identify the engineering artifacts from which it originated.

Complete traceability is mandatory.

---

# Article XXII

Versioning

The CSL Standard shall evolve through explicit versions.

Every released version shall possess:

a version identifier,

a publication date,

a compatibility statement,

a changelog,

migration guidance when applicable.

Every generated Engineering Artifact shall identify the CSL version from which it was produced.

---

# Article XXIII

Backward Compatibility

Backward compatibility shall be preserved whenever technically feasible.

Breaking changes require:

technical justification,

formal approval,

migration strategy,

compatibility documentation,

version increment.

Implementations shall never introduce silent breaking changes.

---

# Article XXIV

Evolution

Engineering knowledge is expected to evolve.

Evolution shall preserve:

engineering intent,

historical context,

traceability,

compatibility whenever possible.

Knowledge shall never be discarded solely because technology changes.

Migration preserves knowledge.

Refactoring preserves knowledge.

Regeneration preserves knowledge.

# Article XXV

Extensions

The Canonical Specification Language is extensible.

Future versions may introduce:

entities,

relationships,

validators,

generators,

compiler capabilities,

engineering domains,

runtime capabilities.

Extensions shall never invalidate existing canonical knowledge.

Extensions shall remain constitutionally compliant.

# Article XXVI

Reference Implementations

A Reference Implementation demonstrates practical conformance with the standard.

A Reference Implementation does not redefine the standard.

supported CSL version,

supported compiler version,

supported validation rules,

known limitations,

conformance declaration.

# Article XXVII

Artificial Intelligence

Artificial Intelligence is an engineering executor.

Artificial Intelligence is not an engineering authority.

Artificial Intelligence may:

analyze,

summarize,

compare,

generate,

refactor,

validate,

recommend.

Artificial Intelligence shall never:

redefine human intent,

override governance,

approve itself,

circumvent safety,

modify Canonical Knowledge without human authorization.

Human authority remains absolute.

---

# Article XXVIII

Governance

The CSL Standard shall evolve through an official governance process.

Future governance shall be defined by CSL RFC documents.

No informal proposal becomes part of the standard.

Ideas become proposals.

Proposals become RFCs.

Approved RFCs become Standard.

The Standard becomes the basis for compiler implementation.

---

# Article XXVIII-A

CSL Foundation

The CSL Foundation is the governing body of the Canonical Specification Language.

The CSL Foundation is responsible for:

maintaining the canonical standard,

approving and rejecting RFCs,

issuing official releases,

designating conformance requirements,

protecting the long-term integrity of Canonical Knowledge.

The CSL Foundation operates according to the following principles.

Decisions are made by consensus of active maintainers.

An active maintainer is any contributor who has participated in at least one RFC review within the preceding twelve months.

Any RFC requiring a formal vote shall require a simple majority of active maintainers.

In the event of a tie the RFC is considered rejected and may be resubmitted with revisions.

RFC approval decisions shall be documented and published as part of the RFC record.

The CSL Foundation shall publish a list of active maintainers with each major release.

The Reference Implementation maintainer (AI-Toolkit) holds a permanent seat.

The governance model may be revised through the RFC process.

---

# Article XXIX

Conformance

An implementation may claim CSL conformance only if it satisfies the mandatory requirements defined by:

this Constitution,

the Language Specification,

the Semantic Model,

the Validation Specification,

the Compiler Specification.

Partial implementations shall explicitly declare unsupported capabilities.

False conformance claims are prohibited.
