# PCC-01 — PERSISTENT EXPERIENCE

**Canonical ID:** PCC-01
**Canonical Name:** Persistent Experience
**Canonical Status:** CANON
**Human Authority:** Owner

---

# 1. Canonical Identity

Persistent Experience is the epistemic organism's preserved lived experience.

It is a distinct epistemic function.

Persistent Experience is not identical to:

- Session;
- Memory;
- Evidence;
- Witness;
- Transformation;
- CSL;
- raw dialogue;
- storage.

Connection between these functions does not imply identity.

---

# 2. Canonical Meaning

Persistent Experience is the project-owned preservation of epistemically significant lived project experience in a form that retains sufficient original context, sequence, provenance, identity, relations, and execution reality for later humans and AI collaborators to understand what occurred without depending upon manual reconstruction by the original participant.

Persistent Experience is not merely a log.

It is not merely terminal history.

It is not merely conversation history.

It is not merely a final artifact.

It preserves the epistemically meaningful path through which the organism and Human Authority lived, examined, corrected, executed, observed, and reached a resulting state.

---

# 3. Canonical Purpose

Persistent Experience exists to prevent epistemic loss.

A project may preserve code, commits, documents, tests, Evidence, terminal output, and current state while still losing the lived path that explains how and why those things came to exist.

Persistent Experience preserves sufficient continuity so that later understanding does not depend upon reconstructing history from disconnected artifacts or upon the original human manually retelling the past.

---

# 4. Experience and Session

Experience and Session are distinct.

**Experience != Session**

Session represents the continuity or bounded interval in which Experience may be lived or used.

Experience represents the persistent lived experience itself.

An Experience may relate to a Session.

That relationship does not collapse their identities.

Process death does not by itself erase the identity of a persistent Experience.

---

# 5. Experience and Memory

Experience and Memory are distinct.

**Experience != Memory**

Experience may contribute to Memory.

Memory may preserve what should remain learned from Experience.

Memory does not become Experience merely because it refers to Experience.

Experience does not become Memory merely because it persists.

---

# 6. Experience and Evidence

Experience and Evidence are distinct.

**Experience != Evidence**

Experience represents something lived and preserved.

Evidence represents verifiable support for claims about what occurred.

Persistent Experience does not authenticate itself merely because it exists or survives restart.

Claims about Experience must remain capable of being supported by Evidence where verification is required.

---

# 7. Experience and Witness

Experience and Witness are distinct.

A Witness may provide compact testimony about a transformation or experience.

Witness does not replace the original preserved Experience.

Derived testimony must not silently become the only surviving account of historical reality.

---

# 8. Experience and Transformation

Experience and Transformation are distinct.

Transformation describes meaningful change.

Persistent Experience preserves what was lived through the relevant path.

They may be related without becoming the same epistemic object.

---

# 9. Experience and Raw Dialogue

Persistent Experience is not identical to raw dialogue.

**Experience != raw dialogue**

Human-AI dialogue may provide epistemically significant material for Experience.

Not every message automatically becomes Experience.

When dialogue materially contributes to project evolution, preservation must retain sufficient attribution and sequence to distinguish human contribution from AI contribution.

Human intellectual initiative must not be erased by later AI reformulation.

AI proposals must not later be represented as Human decisions merely because the Human Authority accepted or executed them.

---

# 10. Experience and Storage

Storage is not Experience.

**Storage != Experience**

Files, tables, serialized objects, databases, repositories, or other physical representations are preservation tissue.

They may contain or represent Experience.

They are not the epistemic identity of Experience itself.

---

# 11. Identity

Persistent Experience must possess stable identity.

When an Experience is legitimately persisted and recovered, recovery must recognize the historical Experience rather than inventing a new Experience in its place.

The required identity invariant is:

**ID_before_restart == ID_after_restart**

Identity must remain inspectable and traceable.

---

# 12. Persistence

Persistent Experience must be capable of surviving process death when its governing policy requires preservation.

Persistence must preserve sufficient identity, provenance, relations, and state for legitimate recovery.

Persistence alone does not create epistemic authority.

**Persistence != authority**

---

# 13. Recovery

Recovery must cross a real persistence boundary.

After restart, the organism must be able to recover the historical Experience without silently replacing it with a newly invented identity.

Recovery must be verifiable.

If identity or integrity cannot be established sufficiently, safe refusal is preferable to false continuity.

---

# 14. Capture Before Interpretation

Epistemically significant Experience must be preservable before later interpretation, compression, sedimentation, summarization, Witness formation, Memory formation, CSL representation, or contextual packaging becomes the only surviving account.

Interpretation may occur.

Compression may occur.

Derived representations may occur.

They must not silently erase the independently reachable historical Experience from which they were derived.

---

# 15. Historical Integrity

Later knowledge must not silently rewrite original Experience.

A failed command remains historically a failed command.

A rejected proposal remains historically a rejected proposal.

A contradiction remains historically visible after resolution.

A Human correction remains attributable to the Human.

An AI proposal remains attributable to the AI.

Corrections and later interpretations must be represented through additional traceable structures rather than falsification of historical Experience.

---

# 16. Proposed Execution and Actual Execution

A proposed command and an executed command are different epistemic facts.

The organism must not infer execution merely because a command was proposed or generated.

Execution claims require execution Evidence.

A command may be proposed, edited, rejected, interrupted, partially executed, executed repeatedly, or executed under a different repository state.

These distinctions must remain representable.

---

# 17. Execution Reality

Where technically observable and epistemically significant, Persistent Experience must be capable of relating to execution reality including applicable information such as:

- executed command or command stream;
- stdout;
- stderr;
- exit status;
- execution order;
- working directory;
- repository identity;
- repository state;
- relevant runtime context;
- active Experience identity;
- Session identity where known;
- relevant actor or tool.

Failures are part of Experience.

Successful execution must not be the only history preserved.

---

# 18. Observation and Interpretation

Observation, interpretation, reflection, and raw execution Evidence are related but distinct.

**Interpretation != historical fact**

The organism must preserve the distinction between:

- what occurred;
- what was observed;
- what was preserved;
- what was interpreted;
- what was inferred later.

Interpretation must not silently become raw Evidence.

---

# 19. Provenance

Persistent Experience must preserve sufficient provenance to explain, where applicable:

- who contributed something;
- what produced it;
- when it occurred;
- which Experience contains it;
- which Session relates to it;
- which Transformation relates to it;
- which Evidence supports relevant claims;
- which artifact resulted;
- whether a representation is original or derived.

Provenance available at capture time must not later be replaced by assumption.

---

# 20. Artifact Relations

Persistent Experience may associate resulting artifacts with the Experience that produced or materially changed them.

Such artifacts may include source files, research, audits, Evidence, tests, commits, configuration changes, CSL, Witnesses, and Transformations.

Association establishes provenance.

Association does not grant the Experience authority over the canonical status of the related artifact.

---

# 21. Protection

Persistent Experience must be protected against illegitimate access, contamination between contexts, uncontrolled modification, unjustified exposure, and recovery into the wrong identity.

Persistence without adequate protection is insufficient.

Protection must preserve the distinction between Experience and the authorities that may act upon it.

---

# 22. Privacy

Persistent Experience must respect legitimate privacy boundaries.

Persistence does not justify indiscriminate capture or exposure.

The organism must preserve only what is legitimate for the relevant Experience and must prevent unnecessary propagation of protected material across epistemic boundaries.

---

# 23. Retention

Not every Experience must be preserved forever.

Retention policy must be explicit and executable.

Persistence without retention control is incomplete physiology.

---

# 24. Forgetting

The organism must be capable of controlled forgetting without falsifying history.

Forgetting must not be accidental corruption or unexplained loss.

Where forgetting legitimately removes Experience material, the operation must obey applicable policy, authority, protection, and Evidence requirements.

---

# 25. Conflict and Ambiguity

Persistent Experience must not invent certainty when preserved representations conflict.

Conflict must remain explicitly representable.

Ambiguity must remain visible when available reality is ambiguous.

The organism must be able to express that it does not know or cannot determine something without additional Evidence when that is the real epistemic state.

---

# 26. Lifecycle and State

Experience must follow defined lifecycle transitions.

Not every state may transition arbitrarily into every other state.

Allowed and forbidden transitions must remain governed by the accepted physiology.

Recovery must not bypass lifecycle integrity.

---

# 27. Coordination and Concurrency

Concurrent or coordinated interaction with Persistent Experience must preserve identity and integrity.

Coordination mechanisms must not silently create multiple contradictory historical identities for one Experience.

When conflict cannot be safely resolved, the organism must preserve the conflict or refuse unsafe continuation rather than inventing certainty.

---

# 28. Migration

Persistent representations may evolve technically.

Representation migration must preserve the epistemic identity and legitimate historical meaning of Experience.

A storage-schema change must not silently redefine the Experience itself.

Unsupported or unsafe representation transitions must be rejectable.

---

# 29. Backup and Recovery

Operational preservation mechanisms may back up and recover Persistent Experience.

Backup copies do not become independent Experiences merely because they are physically distinct representations.

Recovery must preserve identity, integrity, provenance, and applicable protection boundaries.

---

# 30. Operational Observability

The organism must be able to observe the operational health of Persistent Experience sufficiently to determine whether persistence, recovery, protection, and related physiology are functioning.

Operational observability must not redefine Experience.

Telemetry about Experience is not the Experience itself.

---

# 31. Performance

Persistent Experience must remain operationally usable at the scale for which the organism declares it production-ready.

Performance characterization is Evidence about implementation behavior.

Performance results do not redefine canonical meaning.

---

# 32. Deployment Behavior

Persistent Experience must preserve its required identity and persistence behavior across legitimate deployment boundaries for which production readiness is claimed.

Deployment infrastructure is not Experience.

Deployment changes must not silently redefine the epistemic identity of preserved Experience.

---

# 33. Safe Failure

If identity, protection, provenance, integrity, migration safety, or recovery legitimacy cannot be established sufficiently, the organism must be capable of refusing the operation.

Safe refusal is preferable to false continuity.

---

# 34. Human Authority

Persistent Experience does not possess authority merely because it preserves history.

Human acceptance does not become implementation.

Implementation does not become Canon.

Evidence demonstrates.

Human Authority judges where CEP-000 reserves judgment to the Human.

Canonical authority is governed by CEP-000.

---

# 35. Canonical Relationship to Implementation

The current software implementation may demonstrate PCC-01.

The software does not define PCC-01 merely by existing.

Future implementations may change while PCC-01 retains its canonical identity, meaning, purpose, relations, and justification.

Code must conform to legitimate Canon.

Canon must not be silently rewritten to conform to incidental implementation details.

---

# 36. Canonical Justification

Persistent Experience is canonical because epistemic continuity is a requirement of the organism rather than an incidental property of one implementation.

Without it, the organism may retain products of its history while losing the lived epistemic path required to understand those products.

Persistent Experience therefore protects continuity between historical reality, later Evidence, Human judgment, Memory, future interpretation, and continued organism evolution while preserving the distinctions between those functions.
