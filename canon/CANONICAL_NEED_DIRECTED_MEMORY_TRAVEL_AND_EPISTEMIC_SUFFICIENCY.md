# CANONICAL NEED-DIRECTED MEMORY TRAVEL AND EPISTEMIC SUFFICIENCY

**Organism:** AI-Toolkit
**Canonical function:** Need-directed travel through layered Memory
**Human Authority:** Owner
**Human Decision Status:** ACCEPTED
**Canonical Status:** CANON
**Self-Ratification:** NO

---

## 1. Purpose

The epistemic organism possesses layered Memory.

Layering alone is insufficient.

The organism must also possess a legitimate principle governing why it enters
Memory, how far it travels through Memory, when deeper historical ancestry is
required, and when the search may legitimately stop.

Memory research must therefore be directed by an epistemic need rather than by
arbitrary depth, implementation convenience, or indiscriminate retrieval.

---

## 2. Governing Principle

Every investigation of Memory begins from an identifiable epistemic need.

The organism should begin with the relevant representation that is nearest,
most sedimented, or otherwise appropriately available for that need.

It must travel progressively toward deeper epistemic ancestry only as far as
necessary to satisfy the need legitimately.

The depth of Memory research is therefore not an arbitrary fixed number.

Required depth is determined by the unresolved epistemic need.

---

## 3. Epistemic Sufficiency

Memory travel may stop when the available information forms an epistemically
sufficient answer to the need that caused the investigation.

An answer is not sufficient merely because some representation appears to
answer the question.

The strength of the answer must not exceed the Evidence supporting it.

Epistemic sufficiency requires the organism to consider, where relevant:

- whether the need has actually been answered;
- whether the answer possesses adequate support;
- whether relevant provenance is available;
- whether material uncertainty remains;
- whether relevant ambiguity remains;
- whether meaningful conflict remains;
- whether deeper historical reality is required to verify the answer;
- and whether the conclusion would become stronger than its Evidence.

Sufficiency is therefore need-relative and Evidence-bounded.

---

## 4. Progressive Descent

When the current Memory layer is insufficient, the organism must be capable of
continuing toward deeper epistemic ancestry.

The canonical direction may include:

Memory
→ deeper Memory
→ Sedimentation
→ Persistent Experience / Transformation
→ Evidence
→ available historical reality

The exact structures traversed depend on the need and on the ancestry that
actually exists.

The organism must not fabricate a missing layer merely to complete this path.

---

## 5. Conditions Requiring Deeper Travel

Encountering an apparent answer does not automatically terminate Memory
research.

The organism must be capable of continuing deeper when relevant information is:

- insufficiently supported;
- uncertain;
- ambiguous;
- conflicting;
- unconfirmed;
- materially incomplete;
- missing necessary provenance;
- dependent upon interpretation that requires source examination;
- or otherwise incapable of legitimately satisfying the initiating need.

Meaningful contradiction is a reason for examination, not a reason to choose
the most convenient representation.

Uncertainty is a legitimate epistemic state and must remain visible.

---

## 6. Conditions Permitting Stop

The organism should not continue descending through Memory without epistemic
need after the initiating need has been sufficiently satisfied.

Deeper retrieval has a purpose.

Depth is not a virtue by itself.

The organism may stop when further descent is not materially necessary for the
answer, judgment, verification, or other legitimate epistemic purpose being
served.

Stopping at a higher Memory layer does not destroy deeper ancestry.

The path toward deeper origin must remain available.

---

## 7. No Premature Satisfaction

The organism must not stop merely because it encountered the first
representation compatible with the question.

It must not confuse:

- relevance with sufficiency;
- familiarity with truth;
- persistence with authority;
- Memory with Evidence;
- interpretation with historical fact;
- agreement with verification;
- or availability with completeness.

The organism must resist premature epistemic closure.

---

## 8. Legitimate Failure to Find an Answer

The purpose of Memory research is to satisfy an epistemic need when the
available reality permits that need to be satisfied.

The organism is not required to manufacture an answer.

If sufficient support cannot be established after legitimate examination of
the available epistemic ancestry, the result must preserve the actual state.

Legitimate outcomes include, where appropriate:

- UNKNOWN;
- UNCERTAIN;
- AMBIGUOUS;
- CONFLICTING;
- UNCONFIRMED;
- NOT VERIFIED;
- or another explicitly bounded non-answer state.

Absence of knowledge must not be represented as knowledge.

An unresolved need remains unresolved.

---

## 9. Provenance Preservation

Stopping Memory research at any layer must never destroy the path toward the
origin of that Memory.

Derivation must not destroy origin.

A sedimented representation must remain distinguishable from the Experience,
Transformation, Evidence, observation, or other ancestry from which it arose.

When deeper examination later becomes necessary, the organism must be capable
of returning toward that ancestry according to legitimate access rules.

---

## 10. Separation of Epistemic Functions

Layered Memory provides the anatomy through which Memory can possess depth,
relationships, persistence, bidirectional traversal, and ancestry.

The decision that a particular epistemic need requires deeper recall is a
distinct physiological concern.

The organ that evaluates need and epistemic sufficiency may cooperate with
Layered Memory, Evidence, Provenance, Experience, Judgment, CSL, and other
epistemic organs.

Cooperation does not collapse their identities.

Connection does not mean fusion.

No organ acquires another organ's authority merely by participating in Memory
research.

---

## 11. Relationship to Progressive Recall

This Canon establishes the governing principle required by Progressive Recall:

**recall depth follows unresolved epistemic need.**

Progressive Recall must not mean indiscriminate retrieval of all historical
material.

Its purpose is controlled epistemic deepening.

At each meaningful stage it must be possible to determine whether:

- the need is sufficiently satisfied;
- deeper ancestry is still required;
- or the available organism cannot legitimately provide a stronger answer.

The implementation contract of Progressive Recall must be derived from Canon
and must not silently introduce a different stopping principle.

---

## 12. Relationship to CSL / Living Project Image

CSL / Living Project Image may provide a current demonstrable representation
from which an epistemic investigation begins.

Current representation does not replace historical ancestry.

When the current representation is sufficient for the need, unnecessary
historical expansion should not be required.

When the current representation is insufficient, uncertain, contradictory, or
requires verification, the organism must be capable of moving toward the
relevant deeper Memory and historical Evidence.

CSL does not acquire authority to invent missing history.

---

## 13. Relationship to Evidence

No conclusion reached through Memory research may legitimately be stronger
than the Evidence supporting it.

Memory can guide the organism toward what it has learned.

Evidence determines what can be demonstrated.

A Memory representation may be useful and relevant while still requiring
deeper Evidence examination for a particular need.

The organism must preserve this distinction.

---

## 14. Relationship to Uncertainty and Conflict

Uncertainty does not automatically require unlimited descent.

Conflict does not automatically require unlimited descent.

The question is whether the unresolved uncertainty or conflict materially
affects the epistemic need.

If it does, deeper examination may be required.

If it does not materially affect the legitimate answer to the initiating need,
the organism may preserve the uncertainty or conflict explicitly while
stopping the current investigation.

The organism must not erase either merely to achieve apparent completeness.

---

## 15. Minimum-Assumption Boundary

Missing information does not authorize invention.

When continuation requires an assumption, the assumption must remain
distinguishable from established fact whenever it materially affects the
result.

Memory travel exists to reduce unjustified assumption where deeper relevant
ancestry is available.

It does not authorize the organism to fabricate ancestry where none exists.

---

## 16. Human Authority

This Canon governs how the organism researches its Memory.

It does not transfer Canonical Authority to Memory, Progressive Recall, CSL,
Evidence, an AI model, or an automated process.

The organism may retrieve, compare, analyze, expose uncertainty, and form
bounded judgments.

Canonical admission and canonical modification remain governed by legitimate
Human Authority.

---

## 17. Canonical Summary

The governing physiology is:

**Need
→ relevant Memory
→ evaluate epistemic sufficiency
→ if insufficient, descend toward deeper relevant ancestry
→ reevaluate
→ stop when the need is sufficiently supported
OR
→ preserve an explicit unresolved epistemic state when sufficient support
cannot be established.**

Therefore:

**The limit of Memory depth is not a predetermined layer.**

**The limit is the point at which the initiating epistemic need has been
legitimately satisfied to the degree supported by available Evidence.**

If that point cannot be reached, the organism must preserve the unresolved
state rather than invent an answer.

At every stopping point, provenance and the possibility of legitimate future
return toward deeper ancestry must remain preserved.
